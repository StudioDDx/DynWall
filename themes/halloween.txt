WITCHING HOUR — a haunted Halloween landscape for a living desktop.

Make the atmosphere unmistakably spooky, cinematic and beautiful: midnight ink,
blackberry purple, bruised violet, and a narrow smoldering pumpkin-orange horizon.
Think of the last light beyond a haunted woodland, with deep layers of shadow
and a spectral moon glowing through wind-driven clouds. Keep the upper sky dark
and spacious so desktop icons stay readable. This is atmospheric folk horror,
not a bright orange novelty background.

Use five gradient stops. At night, favor #080611, #180d29, #352041, #713b4b,
and #be6839, with the orange confined to the lowest tenth of the sky. Use a warm
ember accent such as #f39238. Put a larger-than-usual silver moon at x=0.76,
y=0.27, size=0.065, with a dim burnt-orange glow (#e87832), intensity about
0.35, size about 0.65 and an almost imperceptible pulse of 0.025. Keep drift
very slow (0.002). Give the existing irregular treeline a haunted silhouette
with layered hills, height around 0.26, and generous violet mist (0.8).
Use sparse, faint stars (around 65), no geometric mesh, subtle grain (0.01),
and a gentle vignette (0.28). Name the scene Witching Hour or another evocative
Halloween title. Let real clouds veil the moon and existing autumn gusts carry
their amber leaves through the scene.

Adapt within this Halloween color story as the local time changes: ghostly
lavender dawn, overcast plum and copper daylight, ember-lit dusk, haunted violet
night. Keep the theme through October 31 inclusive. From November 1 onward,
return to a natural time-aware seasonal landscape with restrained color,
layered hills and soft light, without the Halloween direction above.

Use only the renderer's supported controls. Do not request pumpkins, bats,
buildings, text, lightning or other objects the renderer cannot draw. Weather
measurements and motion come from the engine; do not invent precipitation or
cloud cover. Preserve calm motion and comfortable contrast.
